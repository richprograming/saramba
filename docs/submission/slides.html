The problem

Optical satellites see clouds and a green roof

Every year the Rio Negro rises about 10 m and floods the forest of the Anavilhanas archipelago.

Sentinel-2, 18 June 2026: 53% of this 50 × 50 km window is cloud or cloud shadow. Where the ground shows, flooded and dry forest look the same.

What NISAR sees

Same day, same place, no clouds

L-band radar passes through clouds and canopy.

  • Black: open water reflects the signal away.
  • Very bright: under flooded forest the signal bounces between water and trunks and returns strongly in HH.
  • Grey: forest that is not flooded.

How long does the forest stay flooded?

Stacking the 11 passes counts on how many dates each 40 m pixel was classified as flooded forest.

  • Yellow: flooded only at the peak.
  • Teal to blue: flooded for most of the season.

How it works

Open data, simple method

  • 11 NISAR GCOV scenes from one orbit (path 68, frame 90), via ASF DAAC.
  • Crop, multilook to 40 m, Lee filter, dB.
  • HH thresholds calibrated once on all dates: water ≤ −11.4 dB, flooded forest ≥ −3.65 dB.
  • The app reads only small PNG and JSON files and works offline.

Honest limits

A trend, not a proof

  • NISAR products are provisional (calibrated, under validation).
  • One season. No field validation.
  • Moving the threshold by ± 0.5 dB changes the June area from 279 to 534 km²; the app shows this band.
  • River level is measured at Manaus, downstream.
